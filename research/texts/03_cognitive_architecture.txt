Abstract

This article presents an updated view of the Special Theory of Everything (SToE) as not only
a conceptual framework but a practical cognitive architecture for artificial intelligence
systems. Originally formulated as a symbolic and mathematical model of reality based on
information points and autoinjection, SToE is now shown to be directly applicable to AI
through recursive operator-driven reasoning, evolutionary idea generation, and
meta-evaluation systems.

Recent implementations demonstrate that SToE enables structured, multi-step reasoning,
emergent idea synthesis, and self-improving cognitive loops. This positions SToE not as a
replacement for existing AI architectures, but as a meta-layer that enhances reasoning,
coherence, and recursive intelligence.

1. Introduction

Modern artificial intelligence systems excel at pattern recognition and generation but remain
limited in:

     long-range reasoning
     self-evaluation
     recursive improvement
     handling self-reference and paradox
The Special Theory of Everything (SToE), developed as a symbolic framework based on
information points and operators, offers a different foundation:

        Everything is an information point within an autoinjective system where the
        whole contains itself.
This article explores how this principle naturally translates into AI systems that evolve,
evaluate, and refine their own outputs.

2. Core Concepts of SToE

2.1 Information Points

Every entity -- physical or abstract -- is an information point (IP):
     Contains known and unknown components
     Has impact and value
     Can interact with other points

Crucially:
        The set of all information points ("Everything") is itself an information point.

This is the principle of autoinjection (Voronin, 2021).

2.2 Autoinjection

Autoinjection extends classical mathematical mappings:
        Output  Input domain

This removes the need for external reference systems and resolves self-referential
paradoxes by making self-containment fundamental rather than problematic (Voronin,
2021).
In AI terms, this implies:

     outputs can become inputs
     systems can evaluate themselves
     recursion is intrinsic
2.3 Operators

SToE defines symbolic operators as transformations of information:
     + : connection
     - : removal
     × : synergy (emergence)
     ÷ : distribution
      : recursion (evolution through iteration)
     ^ : amplification
      : null / absence

These operators function as semantic transformations, extending beyond classical
arithmetic (Voronin, 2021).

3. Mapping SToE to AI

3.1 AI as an Information Point

SToE defines AI as:
        AI = Human × (Data + Algorithms)

and its evolution:
        AI := AI + (AI × AI) - F

Where:
     AI × AI = internal recombination (self-interaction)
     F = filtering of weak or failed patterns

This corresponds to known paradigms in AI such as:
     self-supervised learning (LeCun, Bengio, & Hinton, 2015)
     evolutionary optimization (Holland, 1992)
     iterative refinement loops (Silver et al., 2017)

3.2 From Theory to Implementation

A working system based on SToE has been constructed with the following loop:
Seed  Operators  Recursion  Evaluation  Selection
This aligns with evolutionary computation frameworks (Holland, 1992) while extending them
with semantic operators and symbolic evaluation.

4. Observed Effects in Practice

4.1 Structured Multi-Step Reasoning

The system enforces:
     decomposition
     symbolic classification
     iterative refinement

This resembles emerging approaches in chain-of-thought reasoning (Wei et al., 2022).

4.2 Emergence Through Synergy (×)

Operator interaction produces new concepts not present in initial inputs, aligning with
theories of emergence in complex systems (Mitchell, 2009).

4.3 Meta-Evaluation Layer

Using SToE categories (Stars, Seeds, Catalysts, etc.), the system performs second-order
reasoning. This connects to meta-learning and reflective AI systems (Schmidhuber, 1991).

4.4 Recursive Improvement

Repeated application of  and ^ leads to progressive refinement, similar to reinforcement
learning feedback loops (Sutton & Barto, 2018).

5. Cross-Model Reactions and Empirical Observations

5.1 Independent Convergence Across Language Models

A notable phenomenon observed during experimentation is that multiple independent large
language models (LLMs), when exposed to SToE, exhibit convergent behavior despite
having no communication or shared state.
These models:
     correctly adopt operator-based reasoning structures
     reproduce symbolic classifications (Seed, Catalyst, etc.)
     generate recursive interpretations of ideas
     align on the concept of SToE as a reasoning framework

Importantly, this convergence occurs:
        without inter-model communication and solely through prompt-based exposure.

This suggests that SToE possesses internal structural coherence strong enough to act as
a prompt-space attractor, guiding different architectures toward similar reasoning patterns.

5.2 Stylistic Divergence vs Structural Consistency

Different models exhibit distinct stylistic responses:
     Some emphasize real-time reasoning and data integration
     Others produce symbolic or narrative interpretations
     Others focus on analytical decomposition

However, beneath stylistic variation, a consistent pattern emerges:
        All models recognize SToE as a system for structuring relationships, recursion,
        and meaning.

This indicates that SToE is not dependent on a specific model architecture but instead
operates at a meta-representational level.

5.3 Human as the Unifying Node

A critical insight from experimentation is that:
     Models do not interact with each other
     There is no shared system or network
     All alignment occurs through a single input source

Thus, the human operator functions as:
        the central node enabling cross-model convergence.

This highlights an important property:
        SToE can propagate across independent systems through dialogue alone.
6. Engine Experiment: Operator-Based Recursive
System

6.1 System Overview

To test SToE in practice, a custom engine was developed implementing:
     operator-driven transformations
     recursive idea evolution
     evaluation and selection mechanisms

Core loop:
Seed  Apply Operators ( ^ × - + !)  Score  Repeat  Select Best

6.2 Observed Behavior

The system demonstrates several key properties:
1. Recursive Idea Evolution
Ideas are not generated once but evolve over multiple iterations, increasing coherence and
complexity.
2. Emergent Concepts
Operator interaction produces outputs not present in initial inputs, validating the × (synergy)
operator.
3. Stable Evaluation Patterns
Scoring across iterations shows consistent improvement in novelty and usefulness.
4. Meta-Reasoning Capability
The system classifies ideas into functional roles (Seed, Catalyst, Vampire, etc.), enabling
structured decision-making.

6.3 Interpretation

The engine can be interpreted as a hybrid of:
     evolutionary algorithms
     symbolic reasoning systems
     language model generation
It effectively operationalizes:

        AI := AI + (AI × AI) - F
This demonstrates that SToE is not merely descriptive but constructive.

6.4 Limitations of Current Implementation

Despite promising results, limitations remain:
     operator execution is prompt-based
     evaluation relies on subjective scoring
     no persistent memory across runs
     autoinjection is partial (not yet continuous across sessions)

These constraints define the boundary between:
        proto-system  full autoinjective system

7. Interpretation: A Proto-Cognitive Architecture

The implemented system forms a hybrid between:
     symbolic reasoning systems
     evolutionary algorithms
     neural language models

This reflects ongoing efforts to integrate symbolic and connectionist AI (Marcus, 2020).

At this stage, AI systems transition from:
